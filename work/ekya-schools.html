Work/Integrated Marketing

Five campuses, one voice

A year in-house with Ekya Schools and the CMR group: social media for five campuses across Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and Twitter, campaign strategy and content, designer briefs, community engagement, print, WhatsApp campaigns, press and events, so that a K-12 network and its sister colleges sounded like one family wherever a parent met them.

The challenge

Five campuses, four social platforms, and a group that spans a K-12 school network and its sister colleges: Ekya Schools, the CMR pre-university colleges and a CMR school, each with its own parents, students and calendar. The feed had to stay alive every day for all of them, print and events had to land on top of it, and everything had to sound like one family. My job was to run that whole surface: the strategy, the calendars, the writing, the briefs to designers, the replies to parents, and the campaigns that gave the year its shape.

The work

One story, every surface

Social was the daily work; print, events and the website sat on top of it. Everything was written to one voice and sized for the place it would be read.

Social

Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and Twitter for five campuses: monthly strategy and content calendars, the posts themselves, briefs to the designers, and the comments and messages answered in the brand's voice. WhatsApp Business API campaigns ran alongside. The year's set pieces were a city marathon campaign, a Christmas festival campaign and the run-up to FIND Festival '23.

The insights

What held it together

  • Five campuses need one voice and five calendars. The strategy was shared; the posts were not. Each campus got its own events, faces and dates inside the same tone.
  • The daily feed is what makes the campaigns land. A marathon or a festival post works because parents have seen the school every day in between.
  • Integrated communication is the through-line, not any single asset: the same promise on a booth wall, a newspaper insert and a WhatsApp message, sized for where it is read.
